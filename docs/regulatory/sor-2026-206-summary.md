# SOR/2026-206 (Explosives Regulations amendment): plain-language summary

Status: research note, not legal advice. Based on the Canada Gazette Part II page. The page was read in two pieces through an automated fetch, and the middle of the text is not fully visible. Check the official text before relying on any number below.

## Short answer

- **In force:** the general rule in the amendment's coming-into-force section is that it takes effect on the day it is published in Canada Gazette Part II. Publication date: **2026-10-21** (registered 2026-10-09). So most of the amendment is already in force as of today (2026-11-02).
- **Later dates:** fee-related changes start **2027-04-01**. Transport-permit fee items start on the **second anniversary of publication** (about 2028-10-21).
- **Hard limit that matters most:** an unlicensed person may store no more than **20 kg of rocket motors and reloading kits at one time** (section 333.13). This applies to **advanced high-power motors** (impulse above 40,960 N·s, class R.4). It does **not** appear to apply to E and F motors.

## What changes for a hobbyist

Model rocket motors are grouped in the regulation as:
- **R.1**: model motors, plus model reloading kits and igniters
- **R.2**: high-power motors
- **R.4**: advanced high-power motors (new Division 3)

The regulation does not use the letter classes (E, F) that hobbyists use. Your E and F motors are probably in the **R.2** group, but the visible text does not say which letter classes map to R.2. Confirm this with your supplier before you assume.

Points from the visible text:
- **Sellers:** a licence is needed for R.4 motors. Sellers must store them in a licensed magazine, cannot display them, and must keep sales records for at least two years.
- **Unlicensed storage (R.4):** the storage unit must be away from flammable materials and ignition sources, kept clean and dry, and have a posted "Danger — Fire Hazard" sign at least 10 cm high. Motors must be attended when not in storage. A rocket must never be stored with a motor installed (s. 333.14).
- **Unlicensed storage (R.1/R.2):** the rules point to storage in a dwelling or storage unit that meets sections 331 to 333.1 (s. 330(2)). The text of those sections is not visible in what I could read.
- **Transport:** a carrier needs a transport permit for high-hazard explosives on public roads. Permits last three years. Drivers need training certificates that expire within five years. Records are kept at least two years.
- **Age:** the only age rule in the visible text is for the firearms-related licence (must be 18). No age rule for rocket motors was found.

## Storing 9 to 15 E/F motors at home

- **Count limit:** none found. The only numeric cap I could see is the 20 kg cap for R.4 motors.
- **Mass limit for R.2 (likely your case):** not verified. The quantity limits for R.2 storage are in sections 331 to 333.1, which the fetch did not show. Get the mass of your order and check it against the official text before storing.
- **What to do now:** weigh the order (motors plus any reloading kits). Store at home in a dedicated, dry, clean unit away from heat and ignition sources, with the fire-hazard sign if the regulation requires it. Keep motors out of any rocket. Ask the supplier to confirm their classification.
